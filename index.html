Flick windows into place.

Swipe two fingers on a title bar to snap a Mac window to a half, a third or a quarter, fill the screen or send it to another display.

Download for Mac Free and open source
macOS 14 or later
brew install --cask flickbar/tap/flickbar
Try a gesturePick one below to see what it does.

Arrange windows without reaching for the keyboard

Your hand is already on the trackpad. Flickbar turns the title bar into a place to gesture, and leaves the rest of your scrolling alone.

Every common layout is one swipe

Swipe left or right for a half, then keep swiping the same way for two thirds and one third. Swipe up to fill the screen, and down to minimize.

While your fingers are down, a highlight glides to where the window will land and a light haptic tap confirms it. Press Esc before lifting to cancel.

A grid for everything in between

Rest two fingers on a title bar for a moment and a grid appears: three columns, each full height or split into a top and bottom half. Slide to a cell and lift to snap there.

The Dock listens too

Swipe up on an app’s Dock icon to show all of its windows, down to hide it, sideways to bring its next window forward, or pinch in to quit it. On a minimized window’s tile, any title-bar gesture brings it back and snaps it.

Learn it in a minute

After you grant access, a practice window walks you through the main gestures and ticks each one off as you do it. Open it again any time from the menu.

Set it up your way

Choose what each gesture does, give any action a keyboard shortcut, add space between snapped windows, and turn gestures off in apps that need their title bars to themselves.

Hold ⌥ Option to gesture anywhere in a window, or ⌘ to send it to the display on that side. Flickbar speaks English, French and Persian.

Every gesture

Put the pointer on a window’s title bar or a Dock icon and use two fingers. Each of these can be remapped in Settings.

On a title bar
Swipe left or rightLeft or right half; repeat for ⅔, then ⅓
Swipe sideways, then up or downThat quarter
Swipe upFill the screen; repeat for the middle third
Swipe downMinimize (or restore the size from before snapping, if you choose)
Double-tapCenter the window
Pinch outToggle full screen
Pinch inClose the window
Hold ⌘ and swipeMove to the display on that side
Touch, hold, then movePick a layout from the grid
On a Dock icon
Swipe upShow all of the app’s windows
Swipe downHide the app
Swipe left or rightBring the app’s next window to the front
Pinch inQuit the app

Every action can also have a keyboard shortcut. None are set at first, so Flickbar never takes over keys you already use.

Nothing leaves your Mac

No analytics, no account. Update checks are off until you turn them on, and they send only Flickbar’s version number.

Signed and notarized

Releases are signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple, and updates install only if they match Flickbar’s own signing key.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. There’s no trial, paid tier or account. Flickbar is free software under the GPL-3.0-or-later license.

Why does it ask for Accessibility access?

macOS requires it for any app that moves or resizes other apps’ windows. Flickbar uses it only to find the window or Dock icon under the pointer and act on it when you gesture or press a shortcut.

Will it get in the way of normal scrolling?

No. Only two-finger gestures that start on a title bar or Dock icon are used. All other scrolling and zooming passes through untouched, and mouse wheels are ignored.

Do I need a trackpad?

For gestures, yes. Keyboard shortcuts work with any keyboard, so you can use Flickbar with a mouse too.

Pinch or double-tap doesn’t work

Turn on Zoom in or out and Smart zoom in System Settings → Trackpad → Scroll & Zoom. macOS only sends those gestures when they’re enabled.

Which languages does it speak?

English, French and Persian. Flickbar follows your Mac’s language, or choose one in Settings → General → Language.

How do I update or uninstall it?

Choose Check for Updates… from Flickbar’s menu, or turn on daily checks in Settings. With Homebrew you can also run brew upgrade --cask flickbar. To uninstall, quit Flickbar from its menu bar icon and move it to the Trash, or run brew uninstall --cask flickbar.
